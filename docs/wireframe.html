The idea

How it fits together

Two wheels, self-rated. Each spoke breaks into focus areas, each focus area into action plans. Tasks are the fortnightly to-do list, and they may point back at an action plan. The board reviews the movement.

Person │ ├── Wheel of Life 8 fixed spokes, same for everyone │ └── Spoke · Health │ ├── Score 3 now · 5 in 1 year · 8 in 5 years (self-rated, dated) │ └── Focus area · "Low physical energy" │ ├── Action plan 45 minutes gym Challenge: laziness to go │ ├── Action plan 30 minute walk Challenge: not sleeping well │ └── Action plan weekly core work Challenge: body pain │ ├── Wheel of Business spokes you define yourself │ └── Spoke · Sales / Implementation / CSAT / … │ ├── Score │ └── Focus area → action plans │ └── Tasks one list, all boards └── "Draft the CSAT survey" tag: WOB → linked to an action plan target date · status · notes Board = roster + meeting calendar + completed/open counts. Nothing else.
  1. Both wheels are rated by hand. The business wheel never calculates the Business spoke on the life wheel. They answer different questions, so they get separate answers.
  2. Everything belongs to the person, not the board. Move from one board to another and your wheels, plans and task history come with you.
  3. Tags and links are optional. A task stands on its own. Tagging it WOL, WOB or Open, and pointing it at an action plan, is there when it helps and skipped when it doesn't.
  4. The board holds almost nothing. A list of members, a meeting calendar, and two counts per member. That is the whole shared surface.

Task list — open items only, earliest commitment first.

  1. Sorted by target date, which is the date you committed to. The top of this list is what the board will ask about.
  2. The fourth row carries no link and no wheel behind it. Real life produces work that belongs to no plan, and the list says so rather than hiding it.
  3. The Open count is the honest measure. If most tasks are Open items, the wheels are decorating your work rather than driving it. That is worth seeing.
  4. Cancel is a real ending. A task that turned out not to matter is closed as cancelled, not left rotting or quietly deleted.
  5. Tasks are never assigned by anyone else. Only you write your list.

Board dashboard — numbers illustrative.

  1. Counts and nothing more. No titles, no tags, no dates, no plans. The pressure comes from the number, not from reading each other's lists.
  2. The fortnight runs from the last meeting to this one. Boards meeting on different days will count different windows.

  4. A high open count next to a low finished count is the conversation the board is there to have.

Worth discussing

This table is the only thing any member sees about another. If the board wants more visible — say, which spoke someone is working on — that is a decision to take now, not later.

Meeting calendar — every other Thursday, movable when needed.

  1. Dates generate a fortnight ahead and can be moved for festivals, travel or anything else.
  2. Moving a meeting moves the counting window with it, so the dashboard always matches the gap actually worked.

  4. Nothing in the app runs the meeting. Members share their own screens; the app just makes sure everyone arrives with a current list.

Superadmin — boards and rosters.

  1. Boards are independent. Members, meetings and counts never cross between them.
  2. The superadmin cannot read any member's wheels, plans or tasks. The role creates boards and manages who is in them, nothing more.
  3. One chairman per board. For now it is a title, with no extra sight of anyone's data — the chairman can keep the meeting calendar.
  4. A member who leaves keeps everything. Their history moves with them to the next board.
Behind it

Who sees what

The wheels hold health, money and family. The rule is simple, and it is enforced by the database rather than by the screens.

WhatWho can see it
Name, photo, mobileEveryone on the same board
Meeting calendar and notesEveryone on that board
Finished and open countsEveryone on that board — numbers only
Life wheel and its scoresOnly you
Business wheel and its scoresOnly you
Focus areas, plans, challengesOnly you
Tasks, titles, links, notesOnly you

Visibility.

  1. Sharing happens by screen share, on purpose. The member decides in the moment what the group sees, rather than the app deciding for them.
  2. Chairman and superadmin get no exemption. Neither can open another member's wheel.
  3. Nobody can edit anyone else's anything. There is no administrative override on a member's own record.
  4. Honest self-rating depends on this. A Family score of 2 gets written down only if nobody can browse it unannounced.
Behind it

Open questions for the board

Decisions worth taking together before anything gets built.

Is counts-only enough on the dashboard?

Today the board sees two numbers per member. Some boards would want to see which spoke someone is working on, without the task detail. Easy to add now, awkward later.

Should the chairman be able to see anything more?

At the moment the chairman keeps the calendar and sees exactly what every other member sees. If the role is meant to chase people between meetings, it may need more.

Do the eight life spokes stay fixed forever?

Fixed keeps a shared language across all boards. The cost is that a member who wants a ninth area of life has nowhere to put it except the business wheel.

What does a score of 3 mean?

Nothing in the app defines the scale. Ratings six months apart are only comparable if each member reads the numbers the same way each time. The board may want to agree rough anchors.

How is a quiet spoke noticed?

With eight life spokes and nine business ones, no fortnight touches them all. The app could show how long since a spoke last had a task against it, so nothing drifts for a year unnoticed.
